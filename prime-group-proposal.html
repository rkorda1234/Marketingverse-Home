Working together

A proposal for Prime Group, in two phases: starting with the work that moves fastest and costs least.

Marketingverse · August 2026
The approach

Fix and strengthen first. Expand second.

The audit surfaced a lot. Not all of it should happen at once, and most of it doesn't need to.

Phase 1 takes everything currently being done and does it better: the content, the profiles, the reviews, the websites, the reporting. It's the cheapest work with the fastest return, and it costs less than the current arrangement.

Phase 2 is everything the group has never done: creators, concierges, hosts, events, loyalty. It gets funded by what Phase 1 frees up rather than by new budget.

Phase 1 costs less than today and pays for Phase 2 out of the difference.
Phase 1

What's included, monthly

  • Social across all seven venues. Strategy, content calendar, posting, captions. Three posts and four stories per venue per week, consistent, not sporadic.
  • Weekly capture visits. Someone on the ground with a camera every week. Four trips covers all seven venues, since Uncle Liu's, Coconut Room and San Si Wu share a location.
  • Community management. Comments, DMs, WhatsApp enquiries, review responses.
  • Google profiles and review engine. Maintenance, photos, Posts, Q&A, plus the review collection system at the table and on WhatsApp.
  • Website maintenance. Ongoing updates, offers, menus, seasonal changes.
  • Print and collateral design. Flyers, menu changes, event material. Included at no extra cost.
  • Monthly reporting. One view across all seven venues, delivered without being asked for.
Phase 1 · Content

What actually changes about the social

More posting on its own won't move much. The direction is what changes.

From

Plated food. Empty rooms. Static images. Inconsistent posting. Nobody in frame.

To

Full rooms, guests, staff, the kitchen mid-service, the chef explaining a dish, the energy of a Friday night.

  • 40% minimum with people in frame. The single most important change. Every reference account you admire sells the room, not the plate.
  • Majority video. Reels and short-form carry reach; static carries the grid.
  • One engagement post per venue per week. Comment-to-claim offers: a free appetiser, a drink. Cheap, high-reach, and it builds an audience we can reach again.
  • Chef-led process pieces, quarterly batch. Dylan to camera explaining why a dish is what it is, cut with the fire and the knife. One morning produces ten or twelve.

Comment-to-claim offers are structured as genuine giveaways with terms, rather than bare engagement prompts, to stay inside platform policy.

Phase 1 · Production

Two production shoots per venue, per year

Currently there are two shoots a year for the whole group. That's one visual refresh every six months across eight concepts, and it's the reason the feeds look the way they do.

Two per venue per year builds the hero library: menu changes, seasons, campaigns. Batched by location, that's roughly eight to ten shoot days rather than fourteen.

The weekly capture visits are what actually change the feeds. A packed Friday can't be scheduled into a shoot calendar a month in advance. You have to be standing there when it happens.

Production shoots are quoted separately once local crew rates are confirmed. Weekly capture visits are included in the monthly fee.

Phase 1 · One-time

Foundation

  • Website conversion fixes. Clickable phone numbers, real addresses, offers above the fold, dead buttons, plated food on the catering pages, a booking path that isn't a twelve-hour promise.
  • Google profile loose ends. The duplicate phone number, Yallah's contradictory reservation signal, Smash's unconfirmed hours, vendor names appearing in indexed page titles.
  • Prime Kitchen rebuilt properly. Converted to a service-area business, review collection started, enquiry button added, hours corrected. This is the one profile that genuinely needs work.
  • Group brand mark. "By Prime Group" applied across menus, receipts, confirmations, gift cards, footers and bios, with a group web presence behind it, so a curious guest finds something.

A full website rebuild is available and quoted separately, on scope. Not required to begin.

Phase 1 · Measurement

What arrives every month

Three tiers, stated honestly. No agency can attribute every cover to a specific post.

  • Provable: bookings carrying a source tag, click ID or promo code
  • Directional: Google profile actions: calls, direction requests, website taps
  • The plain number: covers against the same period last year

Measurement differs by venue. Cost per reservation for Uncle Liu's, Coconut Room and Prime Kitchen. Profile actions and delivery orders for Lucky Rabbit, San Si Wu, Smash and Yallah, which don't take bookings.

Promotions get restructured. Offers that bring someone in (Celebrations, Happy Hour) measured on covers and new guests. Offers that reward people already in the room measured on repeat visits within ninety days. On a single scoreboard, half of them will always look like failures.

Phase 2

What the savings buy

None of this needs new budget. It's funded by what Phase 1 releases, and it's sequenced so the highest-return work comes first.

First

The referral network

Airbnb and villa hosts, hotel concierges, villa companies, yacht charters. This is where Prime Kitchen's flat result gets solved, and it costs almost nothing to start.

First

Local creators

Micro-creators who live here or visit here. Structured to stay inside work permit rules: comped visits, organic posting, content licensing.

Second

Department of Tourism

They already fly creators in at no cost to businesses. Being on the shortlist is a relationship and a familiarisation dinner.

Second

Prime Passport

One loyalty account across all venues. The gift cards already prove the mechanism works.

Third

Events and programming

Dinner that turns into something: upscale at Liu's, looser at Coconut Room. Subject to licensing and noise checks.

Third

Press

Caribbean Journal, Travel + Leisure, Condé Nast, plus local coverage. Earned press is credibility, ad creative and concierge material at once.

Later

WhatsApp assistant

Hours, menus, allergens and bookings answered instantly across all venues.

Later

Scaled paid media

Once Phase 1 proves what converts, the freed budget goes behind it.

To begin

What we'd need

  • Access. Google profiles, Meta Business Manager, websites, existing ad accounts.
  • A local shooter. We'd hire and manage someone Cayman-based for the weekly visits, a local hire, so there's no work permit exposure.
  • Two numbers. Cover counts by venue, and confirmation of who currently manages the ad spend.
  • A decision on the rebuild. Whether the websites get fixed or rebuilt changes the foundation scope, not the monthly.
Ninety days is enough to judge it.

Profiles cleaned up, reviews climbing on a chart you can see, rooms that finally look full, and a monthly report that arrives without being requested. If that isn't happening, you'll know before we tell you. You'll be looking at the same dashboard.
